Our Story

It started with one cart, one kadai and a corner of the evening market where the lamps came on at six.

Oliver Bennett cooked what he had learned from the market cooks he grew up beside: slow-simmered karahi, kebabs off the coals and jalebi spun straight into hot ghee. There was no sign and no seating, only a queue that grew longer every night and neighbours who brought their own plates.

Four years later the cart became a twelve-seat kitchen with a tandoor built by hand. The recipes stayed the same, and so did the rule Oliver wrote on the first menu board: cook it like family is waiting. Today that kitchen has grown into four dining rooms, and the first copper kadai still hangs above the pass in our flagship.

Our Philosophy

Cooked the Slow Way

We never found a shortcut worth taking. The same patience that fed the first queue still shapes every plate we send out.

  • 01

    Market fresh, every morning

    Our chefs still walk the market at dawn for herbs, vegetables and the day’s best cuts.

  • 02

    Spices ground in-house

    Whole spices are toasted and ground in small batches each day, so every masala tastes the way it should.

  • 03

    Fire and patience

    Charcoal tandoors, sealed dum pots and sauces simmered for hours, never rushed for a busy night.

The People

Our Kitchen Family

Many of the cooks who stood beside Oliver at the cart now run kitchens of their own.

Head Chef Charlotte Bennett, Oliver’s daughter, grew up taking orders at the cart after school. She now leads all four kitchens and still tastes every new dish against her grandmother’s recipe book before it reaches the menu.
